The mission

Give the trades the tools the big outfits already have.

The big outfit in your town has a web site that works, a phone that always gets picked up, and a Facebook page that looks alive. It also has an office to run all that. You have a truck. Groovemark exists to close that gap: I set the tech up, I run it every month, and your business runs smoother and grows while you keep doing the work people actually pay you for.

A plumber with a good web site, a phone that never goes to voicemail and a feed full of real jobs wins work from the plumber without them. Same skill, same truck, same town. The only difference is who got found and who got through.

The goal was never to make you a computer guy.
It’s to make sure you never have to be one.

Where I come from

Fifteen years across the desk from business owners.

Before Groovemark I ran a bank branch for fifteen years. The people on the other side of my desk were plumbers, roofers, electricians and excavators: guys with a truck, a crew and a slow February. I saw who was growing and who was treading water, and it was rarely about who did better work.

The ones growing were easier to find. A web site that looked real. A phone somebody answered. Reviews a stranger could read at 10 at night. The ones treading water did the same quality work and waited on word of mouth. Nobody at a bank was going to fix that for them, and it bugged me for years.

Those years taught me something else. An owner’s problems are nothing like an employee’s. Nobody hands you a paycheck on Friday. You find the work, do the work, bill the work and chase the check, and somewhere in there you’re supposed to have a web site, answer every call and post on Facebook. Helping owners carry that part was the piece of the job I liked best.

So in April 2026 I started Groovemark LLC in Missoula, Montana to do that one thing full time: set up the tech that gets a trade business found, and run it, so the owner can run the business.
